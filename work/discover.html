Case study 02 · Discover

Fix the foundation.

At Discover I unified seven risk products under one design system, then used that foundation to design GRaCe, an AI assistant that cut incidents by 34%.

Project 01 · Design systems

One system.
Seven products.

Discover’s governance, risk and compliance tools had each been built on their own. I mapped the whole ecosystem, merged two design systems into one, and redesigned the products on top of it.

Lead Product Designer & Design Systems Lead1.5 years36-person team · 2 designers

Seven products. Two design systems. One language.

01 — The problem

Every product spoke
a different language.

There was no common foundation, no shared design language and no reusable standards. Users had to relearn every tool, and teams kept rebuilding the same components.

When I use one product and then another, I have to learn a whole new way to work.
I get lost digging through endless documents to figure out what to do — and usually end up asking for help.
I feel like we build the same things over and over again.

The starting point. Seven products, each with its own patterns. Quotes are from user interviews.

02 — See the whole system

First, make the
invisible visible.

I mapped end-to-end workflows across every product to show the overlaps and gaps. With the PMs, I also set a baseline for the cost of fragmentation using incident volume, help tickets and deploy tickets, so the impact could be measured.

System map. Every screen and handoff across the ecosystem, connected.

Research synthesis. Fields, states and pain points tagged across products confirmed where the silos were.

03 — The design system

Two systems in.
One system out.

I audited all 86+ components in the Horizon and Radiant design systems and merged them into one governed library. Each component came with clear rules for Workspace and Core UI, delivered in formats teams could use straight away.

Unified components and a shareable standards guide. Linked templates made adoption easy for PMs and engineers. I followed up with enablement sessions across GRC.

Roadmap. Components prioritized by impact, starting with notifications.

04 — Notifications

Notifications meant
something again.

Users had learned to ignore the noise. I led a notification initiative across GRC that removed 221 unnecessary messages and rebuilt the essential ones (36 across 7 products) on a single content pattern: clear subject, direct action, and a linked record.

05 — Process Central

It wasn’t native. Now it
feels like it always was.

Process Central lived outside ServiceNow, but users expected it to feel like part of the platform. I rebuilt it from the ground up using the new system, so moving between modules now feels like one product.

Before → after. A dense legacy UI became a clear, task-first workspace with a guided stepper and a confirmed completion state.

Project 02 · AI product

GRaCe.

An AI assistant trained on internal GRC knowledge. It gives people precise, task-specific guidance so they can finish their work before it turns into an incident.

Lead Product Designer6 months12-person team · sole designer

01 — The problem

Incidents ran 2.4× higher
than comparable tools.

A root cause study of incident tickets showed that most weren’t bugs at all. 94% were process or user errors: people couldn’t find the information they needed to do the task correctly.

49%
45%
6%
User errorProcess errorProduct malfunction

Those 94% became my use cases. I chose three KPIs to judge success against: incident volume, task completion rate and time on task.

02 — Strategy

Build or buy?

Before designing anything, I compared the cost and risk of each approach so the team could make the call with the full picture.

ServiceNow AI features

Benefits

  • Lower implementation cost
  • Faster time to value
  • Native to existing GRC workflows

Risks

  • Licensing and usage fees
  • Limited model customization
  • Vendor lock-in

In-house model

Benefits

  • Trained on internal GRC data and terminology
  • Full control over behavior and tuning
  • No per-use vendor fees

Risks

  • Higher upfront build cost
  • Ongoing maintenance and governance
  • Requires dedicated ML support

03 — Training the model

A model is only as good
as its data structure.

Align

Ran a workshop with the modeling, data science and PM teams to define what an AI-assisted interface needed to do.

Audit

Led a cross-team audit of every available input, then held weekly sessions to work through gaps and constraints.

Structure

Restructured scattered GRC knowledge into a clear hierarchy, giving the modelers a foundation they could train on with confidence.

04 — MVP

From a blank prompt
to a built task.

A blank prompt makes users do the hard part. GRaCe turns a few simple choices into a ready-to-run task.

Wayfinder

Smart starting points get people from first open to first task fast.

Build a Task

Pick an action, a source and an output. The task assembles itself, and you can attach context before sending.

Clean chat

Once a task runs, it drops into a familiar, quiet conversation view. The primary action sits at the bottom so finishing the task comes first.

05 — Test, then V2

Users asked for a nudge.
So V2 gave them one.

In UAT, people said they wanted reminders and progress check-ins. V2 added smart reminders and achievements, and removed the redundant “Ask anything” entry point.

Simpler home

Less noise, with reminders one tap away.

Smart reminders

Set a nudge, a check-in or a custom reminder right from chat.

Full control

Edit or cancel any reminder, any time.

Achievements

Visible wins that teams can celebrate across GRC.

06 — Results

34% fewer
incidents.

Tasks got finished more often (up 9%) and faster (8% less time), measured against baseline in UAT.

The project was paused, and later sunset, after Discover announced its merger with Capital One.
